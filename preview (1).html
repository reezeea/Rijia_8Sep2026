How the model works

Implementation details, timing conventions and limitations.

Model 2.0.0-SG

Core mechanics

  • Annual correlated asset-class returns are drawn from lognormal distributions using your expected arithmetic return, volatility and correlation assumptions.
  • The portfolio is rebalanced annually to the entered weights or optional glide path.
  • Contributions are approximated half before and half after each year’s return.
  • Retirement spending and one-off real cash flows are entered in today’s SGD and increased with inflation.
  • CPF LIFE and other income are entered as nominal amounts at their start age. Standard CPF LIFE is modelled as level nominal income; Escalating is modelled with 2% annual growth.
  • SRS is kept in a restricted bucket until the selected access and withdrawal start age, then drawn over up to ten years. The entered effective tax rate applies to 50% of each withdrawal.
  • Failure occurs the first time an eligible cash resource cannot fully fund a required retirement cash flow. Negative balances are not allowed.

Important limitations

  • The model is for education and planning, not personalised financial advice.
  • Lognormal returns do not fully capture market crashes, regime changes, liquidity constraints or prolonged valuation extremes.
  • Return, volatility and correlation assumptions are planning inputs—not estimates of what markets will deliver.
  • CPF legislation, retirement sums, contribution rates, interest rates and payouts can change. The Singapore reference values are dated 8 September 2026.
  • The CPF LIFE reference helper uses published 2026 male Standard Plan examples and is not a personal quote.
  • CPF account formation, housing pledges, extra interest, MediSave rules and every tax scenario are not modelled in full.
  • SRS early-withdrawal rules, foreigner concessions, exact progressive tax and investment-specific taxes are simplified.
  • Primary-home growth, lease decay, sale timing, transaction costs and replacement housing needs are not modelled beyond your entered haircut and unlock percentage.
  • Annual modelling is less precise than monthly cash-flow modelling for users very close to depletion.

How to interpret “success”

The success rate is the percentage of simulated paths that funded every required retirement cash flow until reaching the planning age and met any entered terminal target. It is not the probability that reality will follow the model. A plan can still fail because spending, contributions, policy rules, health needs, markets or inflation differ from the assumptions.

Review the model after major life changes and at least once a year. Replace planning defaults with your own data, especially spending, contribution capacity, CPF LIFE payout and housing assumptions.
